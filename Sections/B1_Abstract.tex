\begin{center}
    \begin{large}
        \textbf{Abstract}
    \end{large}
    \begin{justify}
        \begin{normalsize}
            Formulation of itaconic-acid-derived unsaturated polyester
             composites requires the coupled optimisation of polyester, reactive
             diluent, initiator, and particulate-filler contents.
            Here, Bayesian optimisation was used to improve the compressive
             mechanical properties of a calcium-sulphate-filled bio-sourceable
             resin based on poly(propylene glycol itaconate) and dimethyl
             itaconate. 
            Three formulation variables were explored using eight initial
             Sobol samples followed by 16 sequential experiments.
            Separate single-objective routines targeted apparent compressive
             modulus and peak compressive stress and were benchmarked against 
             space-filling MIPT sampling.
            Within the 24-sample budget, Bayesian optimisation identified
             formulations exhibiting an apparent compressive modulus of 
             5,012~\unit{\mega\pascal} and a peak compressive stress of 
             143~\unit{\mega\pascal}. 
            The corresponding MIPT maxima were 4,851~\unit{\mega\pascal} 
             and 140~\unit{\mega\pascal}.
            An otherwise identical styrene-diluted analogue reached 
             7,271~\unit{\mega\pascal}, such that the matched-budget 
             dimethyl-itaconate system retained 69\% of its modulus.
            A subsequent multi-objective refinement, initialised using the
             combined 56-sample dataset, identified a formulation exhibiting 
             5,458~\unit{\mega\pascal} and 165~\unit{\mega\pascal}, 
             simultaneously exceeding the initial empirical Pareto set.
            These results demonstrate the value of sequential optimisation for
             navigating coupled formulation variables in emerging unsaturated 
             polyester composite systems.
        \end{normalsize}
    \end{justify}
\end{center}
